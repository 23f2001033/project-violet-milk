How to use this for a four-person speech. Each section carries a speaker tag — P1 problem P2 method P3 evidence P4 outcome — suggesting who owns it. Roughly: P1 opens on the problem and the case, P2 explains how the tracing and scoring work, P3 covers evidence integrity and the AI boundary, P4 closes on the legal output, cost and the ask. Every figure below is computed by the running system, not aspirational.

One

The problem, stated as a clock P1 problem

A complainant loses money to cyber fraud. She calls 1930. The Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System freezes on bank rails using UTRs, and it is good at that.

In the demonstration case her money leaves the SBI account at 10:31:04 and is USDT on a public blockchain at 10:32:02 — fifty-eight seconds. From that second the helpline can no longer see it.

The officer who must write the chargesheet is not missing a law. Section 94 BNSS 2023 already compels any exchange to produce records; Section 63 BSA 2023 already governs electronic evidence; the PMLA already makes registered exchanges reporting entities. He is missing one fact: which exchange, and which deposit address. That is a capability gap, not a legal one.

Two

Architecture P2 method

One process, one port. The React interface is built and served by the same FastAPI application, so there is no second service to deploy or fail. SQLite is a single file on disk.

A DataSource interface with three implementations. No engine downstream knows which is active, which is why a judge can paste a live address mid-demonstration and nothing else changes.

Three

The seven rules P2 method

Section 63 BSA requires explaining how an electronic record was produced. Arithmetic can be explained to a court; a neural network cannot be cross-examined. That is the entire reason scoring is deterministic.

RulePtsFires when
R1+20Within one hop of the seed
R2+15Funds leave within 240 seconds of arriving
R3+15Interaction with a known mixer contract
R4+15A cross-chain bridge within two hops downstream
R5+10Fan-out to more than five addresses
R6+10Structuring — repeated amounts under a reporting band
R7+15Time correlation with the victim debit. Suppressed when R1 fires, so proximity is not counted twice

Bands: 0–24 LOW, 25–49 MEDIUM, 50–74 HIGH, 75–100 CRITICAL. A test asserts every score equals the clamped sum of its own cited indicators.

Stated limit. The weights are reasoned, not calibrated. There is no empirical basis for twenty points rather than fifteen, and no labelled ground truth exists to measure a false-positive rate against. What is defensible is that every score is reproducible and contestable: an accused can point at R4 and argue the bridge inference, which is impossible against an opaque model.

Four

Evidence: confirmed versus inferred P3 evidence

The single most important rendering rule in the product. A confirmed_bank or confirmed_onchain edge is drawn SOLID. An inferred_correlation edge is drawn DASHED, in vivid amber, and is the most visible mark on the canvas.

In the demonstration case: sixteen confirmed, one inferred. The inference is a thirteen-second gap between a UPI debit and an exchange deposit. It is typed differently in the database, drawn differently on screen, and printed INFERRED on the face of the production order.

A conversion inherits the basis of its weaker leg, so the rupee-to-USDT conversion reads INFERRED even though the on-chain side is proven.

Five

Dilution, and the honest negative P2 method

Proportional haircut, chosen because it makes the fewest assumptions a defence can attack: it says only that a pool mixes. FIFO and lowest-intermediate-balance both require assumptions about ordering.

HopPrior cleanTainted inTraceable share
Victim account—INR 4,70,000100%
Seed wallet05,200 USDT100%
Layering wallet1,2001,80060%
Exchange pool8,0002,000 at 60%12% — NOT FLAGGED

Threshold is 30%. That pool holds real criminal proceeds and the system still reports the trail as ending there. A tool that only ever says guilty is worthless in a courtroom.

Two models, labelled. haircut where curated evidence supplies real prior balances; propagation on live chains, carrying a caveat that says in plain words it measures reach, not proportion — because a public explorer cannot report an address's balance at the moment funds arrived.

Six

The Indian rupee side P1 problem

Most tracing tools start at a wallet address. An Indian officer starts at a bank statement, so this system reads one.

Ingestion

Two shapes are handled. A transfer list names both ends of each movement. A bank statement is one account's ledger: it has a UTR, a date and an amount, and it does not name the counterparty. There is no honest way to invent one, so the counterparty becomes a node identified only by its reference — UTR:420192830192 — explicitly unidentified.

Amounts and dates are parsed defensively: 4,70,000.00 Dr, bare lakh grouping, bracketed negatives, and several date orderings. Naive timestamps are read as IST, not UTC. A row that cannot be parsed is skipped and counted, never guessed at.

Narration parsing

The narration is the only column in an Indian statement that says anything about who received the money. It now yields the rail, the counterparty's virtual payment address, the PSP handle behind it, and the beneficiary name as recorded.

NarrationExtracted
UPI/DR/412345678901/RAHUL K/YESB/rahul@ybl/PaymentUPI · rahul@ybl · Yes Bank · RAHUL K
IMPS/P2A/509812345678/PRIYA S/HDFCIMPS · no VPA · PRIYA S
BY CASH DEPOSITnames nobody

The boundary, and it must be said aloud. A handle names the payment service provider — @ybl is Yes Bank — never the business behind the VPA. Whether an address belongs to an exchange is established by the bank answering the production order, never by reading a string. A test feeds the parser binance@ybl and asserts it still reports only "Yes Bank".

The value is that the order can cite a VPA rather than only a UTR — and "who owns this handle" is a far more direct question for a bank than "who received this reference".

Seven

Conversion trail — which platform, which currency P1 problem

The graph shows movement. It does not separate the two things an officer must write down, so a dedicated engine does.

  • Where value changed FORM. On the demo case: 4,70,000 INR → 5,200 USDT at the exchange deposit address, marked INFERRED, with the rate derived from the two legs at INR 90.38 per USDT.
  • Where it changed HANDS. Every service the money passed through, split into CAN BE SERVED (exchange, bank, UPI handle — they hold customer records) and PASS-THROUGH (mixer, bridge — a contract with no operator to ask). Confusing the two wastes a week on an order nobody can answer.
  • Which rails. bank_inr → ethereum, in the order travelled.

No company is ever named. An on-chain label is not a legal identity, and a test asserts no brand name can appear in the output.

Eight

Where the AI is, and is not P3 evidence

ComponentRoleTouches a score?
Language modelDrafts the officer's narrative paragraph; guesses bank CSV column headingsNo
Isolation ForestFlags behavioural outliers as leadsNo
DBSCANClusters wallets by behaviourNo
Seven rulesProduce every risk figureYes — only these

The proof, not the promise. The anomaly model flags the complainant — she moved the largest single amount, so she is the statistical outlier. Her risk score is zero. If the model influenced scoring, the victim would be the prime suspect. That test is the reason the model is kept off the score.

Every AI feature degrades to a deterministic path. One test deletes the API key and the cache, then generates a complete dossier from the template and the heuristic column mapper.

Nine

Chain of custody and anchoring P3 evidence

Each custody entry hashes the entry before it:

entry_hash = SHA256(prev_hash | id | time | user | action | target)

Altering any row breaks every hash after it, and /audit/verify names the first broken link. Rows order by an explicit sequence column, never by rowid or timestamp. Identity comes from the signed-in session, never from the request body — before authentication existed, the officer's name was a form field.

A document's own SHA-256 is recorded separately, because a file cannot contain its own digest. That detached record is the only way to verify a dossier afterwards.

Stated limit. This makes tampering detectable, not impossible — anyone who can rewrite the whole chain forward from an edit can hide a change. Optional on-chain anchoring closes that by putting the digest somewhere outside our control. It proves a document existed in a given state at a given block time. It does not prove the document is true, and the dossier says so in those words.

Ten

The three documents P4 outcome

Sec 63 BSA 2023 court dossier

Case metadata, the statutory certificate section, the evidence hash register, graph and timeline with confirmed and inferred listed separately, the risk and dilution appendix, limitations, and the document's own digest.

It states what Section 63(4) actually requires — a certificate in the form of the Schedule to the Act, signed both by the person in charge of the device and by an expert, which is the change from the repealed Evidence Act — and says plainly that this document supplies the particulars but is not the certificate and carries neither signature.

FIU-IND suspicious transaction report — DRAFT

Cites the obligation it is made under: Section 12 PMLA 2002 read with Rule 8 of the PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules 2005, addressed to the Director, FIU-IND, Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, with the seven-working-day timeline. It explains why the exchange carries the duty: Virtual Digital Asset providers were notified as reporting entities in March 2023.

filed is permanently false. A test walks the API schema and fails the build if any endpoint appears that could transmit a report.

Sec 94 BNSS 2023 production order — DRAFT

The answer to "what do I do on Tuesday morning". Addressed to the exchange, reciting its own authority under Sec 94(1), with a Schedule of transactions carrying time, direction, amount, counterparty, transaction hash and CONFIRMED or INFERRED on every line. Six categories of records sought: KYC, account status and due diligence, full transaction statement, linked Indian bank accounts and UPI handles, IP and device logs, and any freeze presently operating.

  • Never signed. A schema test fails the build if any route could issue or serve one.
  • Never invents an addressee. The legal name and registered address are placeholders — we hold an on-chain label, not a company's identity, and serving the wrong entity is a real harm.
  • Names nobody when nothing was found. A trace reaching no exchange says so.
  • Ranked by tainted value, so a large clean inflow cannot select the wrong target.

Eleven

The eleven screens P2 method

Sign-in opens a case briefing — the FIR, the complainant, the amount, the address — and nothing else. Every figure is a fact the officer already had. One button, Begin investigation, runs the trace, which is an audited action: a TRACE_RUN entry lands in the custody log before any panel appears.

Graph detail: entity type is carried by shape as well as colour, so it reads on a projector that washes colour out. FOLLOW highlights the route funds took from the complainant — breadth-first along edge direction, which answers "how did money get here", not "what is this connected to". A risk filter dims rather than hides, because removing a node would quietly change the picture. Light and dark themes both supported.

Fourteen

What it does not claim P3 evidence

Every item below is printed in the repository, in the generated documents, or both. Volunteering them is cheaper than being caught.

  • Rule weights are not calibrated, and the false-positive rate is unknown — there is no labelled ground truth.
  • Attribution is fifteen curated labels. We do not compete with commercial attribution at scale. An unlabelled address renders as unknown, never clean.
  • Bitcoin is not supported. The model is account-based; Bitcoin is UTXO and needs a different graph plus a change-address heuristic that is itself a source of error.
  • Bank clocks and block clocks are not the same clock. Correlation across them is a lead, drawn dashed for that reason.
  • Chain reorganisations are not handled. A trace records what the explorer returned at that moment, which is why the custody log timestamps it.
  • The statutory wording is unreviewed by a legal practitioner and says so on its own face.
  • The interface is English only.
  • Not affiliated with Chandigarh Police. Stated in the README and in a permanent banner on every screen.
